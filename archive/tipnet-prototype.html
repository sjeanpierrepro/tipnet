Check my accuracy

When a paycheck lands, enter its amount. TipNet compares it with what it predicted for that pay period and adjusts your rates so the next estimate is closer.

From your paystub

Use one recent stub. Enter the numbers for that pay period, not year-to-date. Your numbers stay on this phone.

Fill in the end date, the shift count, or both. The end date keeps each night on the right paycheck. The shift count splits your benefits evenly per shift. Both together give the closest estimate.

Everything before deductions: all hourly pay, card tips and declared cash tips.

Deductions

Add a row for each line in the deductions part of your stub, using the dollar amount for that pay period. Pick whether it changes with your pay, like taxes, or stays the same every check, like health insurance.

Rates of pay

Add a row for each kind of pay on your stub. The first row is your main rate and fills in on every night automatically. Hourly and per-shift pay are part of the total you enter each night. Flat amounts, like a bonus, are added on top.

Put TipNet on your home screen

TipNet runs in any browser. Add it once so it opens like an app.

iPhoneSafari → Share → Add to Home Screen
AndroidChrome → ⋮ menu → Add to Home screen
MacSafari → File → Add to Dock
WindowsEdge → ⋯ menu → Apps → Install this site as an app

Always open TipNet the same way. Your phone can keep the home-screen version and the browser version as two separate copies with different nights saved. If you switch, move your data with a backup code below.

Backup

Your nights live only in this browser. Copy a backup code now and then, and paste it here on a new phone to restore.

TipNet gives estimates, not tax advice. Social Security and Medicare apply to every tip dollar. Federal tax taken from your tips may partly come back at tax time under the federal tip deduction, depending on your situation.
